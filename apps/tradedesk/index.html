What it does

Customers, jobs, quotes and invoices for one person with a van. A job is a title and, if you like, a customer; lines and tax are worked in whole cents, with tax taken once on the taxable lines. Raise the invoice from the job and its amounts are copied, so the invoice you sent stays as sent. Numbers continue your own sequence, and a voided invoice keeps its number. Your payment instructions print on every invoice. Write rough notes and it drafts the scope of work for the customer to sign; when they ask for more, it drafts the change order with the price and the days it adds, or says the request is already in the scope. Ask an electrical code question and the answer cites the NEC article; when it cannot name one, it says so and tells you what to look up. Log each decision on a job as it is made: what was decided, who decided it, how they said it and on what day, against the scope as it stood. A logged decision is never edited, only struck with the reason, so the record holds up if a change is ever argued about. From it, Trade Desk writes the weekly update to your customer: what was decided, what was signed, and what you are still waiting on.
